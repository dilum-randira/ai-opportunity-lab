# Opportunity brief — 2026-10-10

## Decision

**Investigate OrderPilot:** an approval-first order-intake assistant for micro social-commerce merchants. Build a tiny offline, deterministic draft parser before considering any AI model or messaging API. This is a **hypothesis**, not validated demand or a novel invention. The real product opportunity, if confirmed, would be reducing repetitive manual order entry while retaining merchant control.

## Evidence and what it does *not* prove

1. **OECD, 2026-04-13:** Survey of more than 2,000 SMEs across 12 OECD countries finds strategic AI integration uneven; time, cost, skills, and cybersecurity remain barriers. This supports exploring narrow workflow tools, **not** specific Sri Lankan demand. https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/empowering-smes-in-the-age-of-ai_bf5a9816-en.html
2. **Small Firms Association (Ireland), 2026-08-10:** 92% reported using generic AI tools but 30% customized AI; lack of technical expertise and time were common barriers. Survey population is Ireland, not the target market. https://www.ibec.ie/sfa/news-insights-and-events/news/2026/08/10/small-firms-association-sound-alarm-over-shallow-ai-adoption
3. **Bluevine US SMB survey, 2026-07-15:** 82% reported at least one barrier to deeper AI use; data security and accuracy were prominent concerns. A vendor survey is directional evidence, not independent proof of market size. https://www.bluevine.com/blog/small-business-ai-trends-report-2026
4. **OWASP, 2026-10-08:** Recent agentic security roundup reinforces strict tool boundaries, least privilege, and human oversight. It is a security rationale, not proof of demand. https://genai.owasp.org/2026/10/08/genai-and-agentic-ai-exploit-roundup-q3-2026/

## Alternatives and competition

- **Wati** already offers official WhatsApp API access, team inbox, automation, AI features, and additional message charges. A new entrant cannot claim the category is empty. https://www.wati.io/en/pricing/
- **Manychat** supports WhatsApp automation as an additional paid capability; buyers may already have sufficient tools. https://manychat.com/product/whatsapp
- **respond.io** also addresses WhatsApp business workflows and notes provider, seat, and per-message costs; confirm current prices before building a financial model. https://respond.io/blog/whatsapp-business-solution-provider
- **Manual WhatsApp Business + spreadsheet/POS** is a zero-new-software-cost substitute and may remain the best option for very low-volume merchants.

**Possible differentiation to test, not assert:** extremely simple approval-first order drafts, local SKU/price support, no autonomous customer messaging, and low onboarding effort for small sellers. No feature moat has been demonstrated.

## Commercial hypothesis

- **Target buyer:** a Sri Lankan or other small social-commerce seller handling enough daily orders to make manual copying painful; initial geography is a testable choice, not a verified demand signal.
- **Pain hypothesis:** time lost transcribing SKU/quantity messages, missed items, duplicate entries, and manual totals.
- **Offer hypothesis:** setup/onboarding plus a monthly subscription for review-first order capture; a bespoke integration service could be an earlier revenue route.
- **Pricing experiment (not an observed market price):** ask whether LKR 2,500–7,500/month plus an optional LKR 5,000–15,000 one-time setup would be acceptable *after* demonstrating measurable savings. Do not quote as established willingness to pay.
- **Cost estimate:** local offline v0 requires Python standard library and no paid APIs; marginal local demo API cost is zero. Real service would incur hosting, backups, customer support, model inference if used, and official WhatsApp/provider messaging fees. Exact unit economics cannot be calculated before message volumes and vendor choices are known.
- **Distribution hypothesis:** opt-in conversations with a handful of merchants and a private demo. No unsolicited outreach or production connection without approval.

## Smallest customer validation experiment

Ask permission to interview **five** merchants. For each, record approximate orders/day, minutes per manual order, error/duplicate frequency, current tools, desired approval workflow, and whether they would pay for a limited pilot. Demonstrate using synthetic or consented redacted messages only. Track a baseline and measure time saved; do not treat a compliment as willingness to pay. A good go/no-go signal is at least two merchants willing to try a supervised pilot and at least one willing to discuss a paid price; this is a proposed criterion, not a guaranteed outcome.

## Engineering deliverable

Implemented a standard-library-only offline parser with catalog-backed decimal totals, quantity bounds, ambiguous-line flags, batch duplicate checks, human-review statuses, and synthetic fixtures. Tested via python -m unittest discover -s tests -v. This is **not yet AI** and must not be described as a deployed product.

## Next iteration (only after feedback)

1. Interview or recruit willing merchants with the user's approval; test whether order entry is the highest-value problem.
2. Add a tiny local approval/rejection UI and durable idempotency, with tests and no external actions.
3. If free-text extraction is truly needed, benchmark an LLM against annotated, consented samples; gate every extracted line with deterministic validation and human review.
4. Recalculate pricing and gross margin using real volumes and vendor quotes before considering any public deployment.
